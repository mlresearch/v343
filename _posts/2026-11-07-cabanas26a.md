title: Accounting for Data Uncertainty in Counterfactual Reasoning
section: papers
abstract: Structural causal models with discrete variables are a class of probabilistic
  graphical models used for causal and counterfactual reasoning. In the presence of
  observational (i.e., non-experimental) data, some queries in such models are non-identifiable
  and therefore cannot be computed exactly. Instead, several methods derive bounds
  on non-identifiable queries by solving linear programming problems. However, these
  methods usually assume a precise specification of the data distribution. In this
  work, we consider a more realistic scenario in which uncertainty in the data is
  represented through an interval-valued specification of the data distribution. We
  formulate the corresponding linear programming problem under this setting, which
  covers both identifiable and non-identifiable counterfactual queries under data
  uncertainty. We show how a recently proposed divide-and-conquer algorithm for bounding
  counterfactual probabilities (Bj{ø}ru et al., 2025) can be adapted to such imprecise
  specifications of the data distribution without a substantial increase in computational
  complexity.
