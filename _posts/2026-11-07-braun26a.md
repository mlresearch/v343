title: Counterfactual Fairness under Hidden Confounding
section: papers
abstract: Designing algorithms that are non-discriminatory has attracted major attention
  in recent years. It has been shown that a causal perspective is necessary to mitigate
  unfair discrimination. However, little attention has been paid to how algorithms
  can make fair decisions under hidden confounding. In this paper, we study counterfactual
  fairness in the presence of hidden confounding and make two contributions. First,
  we show that under commonly adopted assumptions, counterfactual fairness is invariant
  across all causal models consistent with the observed data and causal ordering of
  the variables. In particular, even in the presence of hidden confounding and non-identifiability
  of the underlying causal model, all compatible models yield the same counterfactual
  fairness conclusions. Second, we show how instrumental variables, a tool commonly
  used in causal inference to address hidden confounding, can help to design fair
  predictors when the protected attribute is unobserved. Together, our results extend
  the scope of causal fairness methods to more realistic settings where both confounders
  and sensitive attributes may be partially or entirely unobserved.
