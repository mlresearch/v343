title: What to Check Next? Bayesian Networks and Rollout-Based Lookahead for Quicker
  Inspections
section: papers
abstract: Regulatory inspections, such as labour inspections, take time and money.
  Time and money are always limited. Inspectors often work with checklists. It could
  save a lot of time if they could focus first on checklist items that are likely
  to be non-compliant with the law. We implement a method that can guide an inspector
  in the field by suggesting which item on the checklist they should inspect next,
  based on their answers to previous checklist items. To do this, we train a Bayesian
  Network model on filled checklist data from the Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority.
  We test a myopic policy that always picks the item that is most likely to be non-compliant
  against a policy that looks one step ahead. The lookahead policy computes for each
  candidate item the expected value ($\gamma$-discounted returns) of inspecting it
  under a fixed policy that no longer updates probabilities, also known as a rollout.
  The expected value under the fixed policy is cheaply computed in closed form. We
  find that this lookahead policy increases performance greatly over the myopic policy,
  halving the catching times of non-compliances from roughly $5$ to $2.7$ checks (out
  of a maximum of $14$). The Bayesian network is however slightly outperformed across
  the board by a Binary-Relevance Naive Bayes model intended as a baseline.
