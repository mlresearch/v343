abstract: 'We present the first systematic evaluation of exact Bayesian-network inference
  in frontier reasoning large language models (LLMs). We introduce BNqMark, a benchmark-generation
  framework for exact inference in discrete Bayesian networks, and evaluate BNqMark-20,
  a benchmark instance comprising 78 procedurally generated binary networks and 434
  conditional probability queries with exact ground truth. Across 9 frontier reasoning
  LLMs and 2 evaluation protocols, we find that current models can solve many exact
  inference queries on small and medium Bayesian networks, especially by externalizing
  inference as code. Several models correctly answered queries whose exact solution
  would require more than 13,000 arithmetic operations under variable elimination,
  rising to 68,000 in the code-generation protocol. Model capability degrades as inference
  complexity increases: graph and query complexity are both useful predictors of success
  after controlling for prompt length, network size, and model. These results position
  Bayesian networks as a controlled testbed for studying probabilistic reasoning in
  LLMs and motivate future work on richer and more challenging probabilistic inference
  settings.'
