title: On Computing Total Variation Distance between Markov Logic Networks
section: papers
abstract: The total variation (TV) distance is a fundamental metric for quantifying
  the difference between probability distributions. Bhattacharyya et al. (2024) showed
  that computing the TV distance between two directed graphical models is, in general,
  intractable. We extend this line of research to Markov Logic Networks (MLNs), a
  prominent class of statistical relational models that combine first-order logic
  with probabilistic graphical models. Specifically, we establish both tractability
  and hardness results regarding the computation of the TV distance between two MLNs.
  On the positive side, we reduce TV distance computation between MLNs to probabilistic
  inference, enabling the use of lifted inference techniques. Thereby, we identify
  classes of MLNs for which TV distance computation is tractable. On the negative
  side, we prove that, in general, the TV distance computation between two MLNs is
  intractable. In addition to these theoretical results, we empirically validate that
  soft rules with moderate weights can approximate hard rules, and we also present
  an application of the MLNs TV distance in benchmarking graph neural networks on
  synthetic data.
