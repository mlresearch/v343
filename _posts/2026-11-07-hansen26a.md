title: Using P&IDs and Process Data To Construct Bayesian Networks For Anomaly Detection
section: papers
abstract: Industrial processes are often safety-critical and consist of multiple interacting
  components. The complexity of such systems, combined with the scarcity of faulty-operation
  data, makes the construction of reliable anomaly detection models challenging. To
  address this issue, we investigate how qualitative information in technical process
  documentation - specifically Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams (P&IDs) - can guide
  the construction of Bayesian networks for anomaly detection. P&IDs describe the
  instrumentation and piping of industrial systems, including the placement of sensors
  and actuators and their interconnections. We show how this structural information
  can be exploited to decompose an industrial process into smaller subsystems for
  which local models can be specified or learned independently. For anomaly detection,
  we employ a conflict-based measure that quantifies inconsistencies in observed process
  data. We adapt existing threshold calibration and smoothing concepts to the conflict-measure
  setting, introducing three sequential filters that balance false-alarm frequency
  against detection latency for true anomalies. The proposed methodology has been
  integrated into PredictIT, a predictive maintenance platform for industrial processes
  and systems. Experiments on a simulated formaldehyde production plant demonstrate
  that P&ID-informed models outperform corresponding full-system models, yielding
  lower false positive rates and reduced detection latency.
