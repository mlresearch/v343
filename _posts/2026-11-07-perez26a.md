title: Tensor Train Decomposition for BN2A Structure Learning
section: papers
abstract: 'BN2A models are bipartite Bayesian networks with binary hidden and observed
  variables, characterized by Noisy-AND conditional probability tables with leak and
  penalty parameters. A fundamental challenge in BN2A modeling is identifying the
  bipartite graph structure, the Q-matrix in cognitive diagnostic models, from observed
  data alone. Existing tensor-based identification methods face the curse of dimensionality:
  the joint probability tensor has $2^J$ entries for $J$ observed variables, and constructive
  algorithms such as that of Gu (2026) require materializing it explicitly. We bridge
  BN2A structure learning and tensor network methods by showing that BN2A probability
  tensors have Tensor Train (TT) rank at most $2^K$, where $K$ is the number of hidden
  variables, reducing storage from $O(2^J)$ to $O(J \cdot 4^K)$, a bound that follows
  from a known equivalence between BN2A and Restricted Boltzmann Machines. Building
  on this representation, we show that Gu’s rank tests can be performed directly on
  TT cores via core contractions, without ever materializing the full tensor. Empirical
  validation on simulated data with $J = 15$ and $K = 3$ confirms the rank bound and
  achieves perfect structure learning; we further characterize the sample-size regime,
  showing that recovery is robust for $n$ as small as $30%$ of $2^J$. We also report
  two honest explorations of the boundaries of the approach: greedy mode ordering
  reduces the TT parameter count of the population tensor by $13%$ and, once an order-sensitive
  ambiguity in pure-item detection is resolved, composes fully with Gu’s rank tests,
  although the storage benefit vanishes on noisy empirical tensors; and Gu’s Step 2
  itself degrades as $J$ grows with $K$ fixed, a limitation of the underlying algorithm
  rather than of the TT representation.'
