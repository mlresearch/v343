title: Finite Dimensional Approximations of Incentive Functions for Causal Incentive
  Design Models
section: papers
abstract: Single-stage principal-agent problems with bilateral private information
  can be represented as additive-noise Causal Incentive Design Models (CIDMs), where
  an incentive policy $\gamma$ acts as a functional intervention on a function-valued
  variable $\Gamma$. This research extends the Causal Incentive Design framework to
  address the computational challenge of densely sampling CIDMs when the intervention
  space $\Gamma$ is a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). This challenge arises
  because RKHS feature maps are typically infinite-dimensional, while CIDM simulation
  requires that each incentive policy be instantiated as an explicit callable function
  and evaluated within a structural equation involving the follower’s best-response
  optimization. To overcome this, quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) and MC Random Fourier Features
  (RFF) frameworks are used to construct finite-dimensional approximations of RKHS-valued
  incentive functions and to densely sample CIDMs. The developed samplers are evaluated
  on five distinct CIDM instantiations. The experiments compare MC-RFF and QMC-RFF
  in terms of the approximation error of the RKHS kernel and the subsequent estimation
  of the causal policy-value target $V(\gamma)$ using Functional Kernel Ridge Regression
  and a Functional Multi-Layer Perceptron, benchmarked against an analytical Gaussian-smoothed
  baseline.
