abstract: 'Timescale Graphical Event Models (TGEMs) provide a continuous-time probabilistic
  framework for representing dependencies among multivariate point processes using
  a directed graph, where each node corresponds to an event label and each edge specifies
  how occurrences of a parent event label influence the conditional intensity of a
  child event label over future time intervals, called timescales. Although TGEMs
  have been mainly investigated from a modeling and learning perspective, the practical
  behavior of sampling-based inference methods in these models remains insufficiently
  characterized. In this paper, we empirically compare three sampling strategies for
  probabilistic inference in TGEMs: forward sampling, Poisson Superposition Importance
  Sampling (PSIS), and auxiliary Gibbs sampling. We evaluate these methods on episodic
  sequence queries with incomplete and imprecise evidence. We examine the consistency
  of probability estimates, convergence behavior, and sensitivity to the rarity of
  the evidence. Our results provide a comparative experimental framework for assessing
  the strengths and limitations of each strategy and offer practical guidance for
  method selection in TGEM-based inference.'
