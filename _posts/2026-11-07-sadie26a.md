abstract: 'Inference in probabilistic graphical models (PGMs) is sensitive to the
  choice of graph structure used for message passing. While junction trees allow for
  exact inference, their use is practically infeasible due to exponential blow-up
  in cluster sizes. Loopy-structured PGMs, like factor graphs or cluster graphs, are
  computationally tractable alternatives, but the gap between the performance of such
  graphs and the ideal performance of junction trees still exists. We propose "shapeshifter",
  an algorithm that dynamically restructures a cluster graph during inference, by
  merging cluster pairs based on disagreement and redundancy criteria. We evaluate
  on two distinct domains: error correction and constraint satisfaction. The results
  show that shapeshifter offers a practical middle ground: improved inference when
  compared to existing PGM-based approaches—closing the performance gap by a substantial
  margin—without the prohibitive computational expense of junction trees.'
