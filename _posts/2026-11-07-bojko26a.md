title: Augmenting Bayesian Networks with Categorical Principal Components
section: papers
abstract: We propose a method for learning interpretable Bayesian networks (BNs) over
  high-dimensional categorical data by augmenting the original variable set with discrete
  components derived from expert-defined groups of variables. Because the components
  are computed deterministically and entered as fully observed columns, the augmented
  dataset defines a BN in a curved exponential family, making standard BIC asymptotically
  valid for joint model scoring. To enable a fair comparison of models with different
  numbers of component nodes — which induce different model classes over the original
  variables — we introduce a modified BIC that evaluates marginal likelihood over
  the original variables while retaining the full parameter penalty. We show that
  this criterion is conservative relative to the true Geiger-Heckerman effective dimension
  and establish when the marginal and joint criteria agree asymptotically. We compare
  PCA-derived components (applied to ordinally coded variables) with Multiple Correspondence
  Analysis (MCA), the discrete analog of PCA, and show theoretically and empirically
  that MCA is the more natural choice. Experiments on the Czech Attitude Barometer
  dataset demonstrate that MCA models achieve the best marginal BIC as well as the
  best held-out marginal log-likelihood across all augmented and non-augmented models,
  yielding a sparse, stable, and sociologically interpretable network.
